# Shared Research Rules

These rules apply to both the Web baseline and the Web + SIJI condition.

1. Product existence does not establish qualification, mass production, shipment, order, delivery, or revenue.
2. Structural upstream/downstream position does not establish a direct customer/supplier transaction.
3. Order does not establish delivery; delivery does not establish revenue.
4. Current-platform facts and next-generation-platform facts are kept separate.
5. Product generations are not merged merely because they serve a similar function.
6. Facts, industrial/technical relationships, agent analysis, unknowns, conflicts, and unsupported claims are expressed separately.
7. Key factual claims and key relationships require traceable sources.
8. Conditional conclusions must state the supporting path, necessary preconditions, relevant time window when justified, alternative explanations, and invalidation conditions.
9. A graph-reachable path is not automatically a causal propagation path.
10. Newly discovered objects should be followed when they materially affect the user's question and research budget.
11. Repeated rediscovery of known identity/context should be avoided unless verification is necessary.
12. Commercial-stage timelines distinguish release, sample, validation, qualification, mass production, capacity expansion, order, shipment, delivery, and revenue.
13. Industry-ramp evidence and securities-market expectation are separate judgments.
14. A product opportunity is not automatically a company-level earnings or valuation conclusion.
15. Market analysis requires independent, contemporaneous and comparable market evidence.
16. Missing or incomparable market data should produce an explicit “not established” result rather than a forced ranking.
17. Agent analysis must be labeled as analysis and must not be rewritten as a sourced fact.
18. When evidence remains insufficient, preserve the unresolved frontier rather than inventing closure.
19. Stop when the research objective is met or remaining questions can only be honestly retained as unknown/unsupported within the budget.
20. Every major conclusion should end with a concrete next-verification step that explains what new evidence would change the conclusion.
