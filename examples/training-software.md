# Research Example — Distributed Training / Data-Loading Software

**Scope:** a narrow public example showing how SIJI keeps company identity, product identity, industry position, evidence, and source provenance separate.

## Research question

Within this example scope, which company/product identities can be traced to original public sources without turning product existence into unsupported commercialization claims?

## Research scope

- Chain context: `ch20_training`
- Focus: public product/software facts
- Coverage: intentionally narrow; not a complete map of distributed-training or data-loading software

## Products / positions

| Company | Product / entity | SIJI position | Atomic fact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ByteDance | BytePS — `entx_bytedance_byteps` | `np_20_003` | `af_r17_bytedance_np_20_003` |
| Uber | Petastorm | `np_20_001` | `af_r17_uber_np_20_001` |
| Activeloop | Deep Lake-related data-loader entity | `np_20_001` | `af_r26_activeloop_np_20_001` |

For BytePS, the source describes it as a distributed DNN training framework supporting multiple deep-learning frameworks and TCP/RDMA transports.

## Commercial facts

This example establishes product/software facts only. It does **not** establish:

- mass production;
- customer certification;
- a customer/supplier transaction;
- an order or contract;
- shipment or delivery;
- revenue contribution;
- deployment at a named customer.

Those questions require separate evidence.

## Unknowns / conflicts

Missing commercial facts in this example mean “not established in this declared scope,” not “does not exist in reality.”
